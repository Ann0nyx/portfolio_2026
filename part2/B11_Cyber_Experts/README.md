# B11 - Talk to 2 cybersecurity experts from the industry and find out their latest projects

## 1) Discussion with Mark Tearle – Senior Systems Administrator

### Description

I spoke with Mark Tearle during a tech talk held at the University Computer Club clubroom. Mark works as a Senior Systems Administrator at the Queensland Cyber Infrastructure Foundation, a joint venture supporting Queensland universities.

## Latest Projects Discussed

### 1. BioPlatforms Australia Data Portal

Mark explained that one of his current projects involves working on a data portal for BioPlatforms Australia. His role includes:

- system administration
- maintaining backend infrastructure
- coding new application features
- transferring large genomic datasets to Amazon S3 storage

The platform supports researchers by managing and publishing genomic data securely for scientific use.

### 2. Square Kilometre Array (SKA) Radio Telescope Project

Mark also discussed his work consulting for the Square Kilometre Array Observatory project being constructed in Western Australia. His responsibilities involve:

- fibre optic infrastructure
- networking project management
- coordinating large scale communication systems

He explained that the project involves massive fibre-optic deployments and high-speed networking infrastructure used to support over 151,000 antennas and large-scale scientific computing systems.

## Knowledge and Advice Gained

During the discussion, Mark also provided advice regarding cybersecurity careers, certifications, networking fundamentals, Linux systems and industry pathways. He explained the importance of understanding how attackers compromise systems, learning defensive controls such as MFA, building strong networking and Linux knowledge and gaining practical experience alongside university studies.

He also discussed how certifications can support career development but emphasised the importance of practical technical understanding.

## Reflection

This discussion gave me valuable insight into real-world infrastructure, cybersecurity operations and large scale research technology projects. Learning about projects involving secure data management, networking and fibre infrastructure helped me better understand how cybersecurity and systems administration are applied in industry environments. The conversation also gave me useful career advice regarding certifications, technical skills and future cybersecurity pathways.

## 2) Discussion with Wellington Mavhunduke – Fraud Analyst at Bankwest

### Description

I spoke with Wellington Mavhunduke, who currently works as a Fraud Analyst at Bankwest. He has worked in fraud investigations and financial crime analysis for the past two years. Prior to this, he worked as a SOC Analyst at Idoba, where he gained experience in threat monitoring, incident response, and security operations. He also holds a degree in Cybersecurity and Digital Forensics from Murdoch University.

## Latest Projects Discussed

### 1. Detecting and Preventing Mule Accounts

Wellington explained that one of his current projects involves investigating the increasing number of mule accounts being created within the banking system. The project focuses on identifying how attackers bypass onboarding procedures to create fraudulent bank accounts used for money laundering and financial crime.

He discussed how attackers attempt to bypass biometric verification systems and use fake identification documents or false addresses during account creation. His team is currently improving detection models and verification systems to identify suspicious onboarding attempts before accounts are successfully created.

The project includes:

- detecting fake identities and fraudulent account creation
- identifying weaknesses in biometric verification systems
- validating address legitimacy
- improving fraud detection tools and onboarding models
- monitoring suspicious money transfers between accounts and overseas destinations

## Collaboration Between Banks

Wellington also explained that part of the project involves improving collaboration between financial institutions to better flag suspicious transactions and verify sources of funds. This helps reduce the use of banking systems for financial crime and scam-related activity.

## Outcomes and Progress

He stated that the team has already reduced fraudulent onboarding rates by approximately 25% compared to previous months, where a large percentage of newly created accounts were identified as fake mule accounts used to quickly move money before disappearing.

## Reflection

This discussion provided valuable insight into how cybersecurity and fraud detection are applied within the banking industry. Learning about biometric bypass attempts, fraud analysis, financial crime investigations and inter-bank collaboration helped me better understand the practical challenges organisations face when securing financial systems and protecting customers from scams and money laundering activities.
