# B5 - Attend 2 cybersecurity related talks/seminars

## 1) Guest Lecture: Fraud Detection in the Real World - From Cybersecurity to the Banking Sector

I attended a guest lecture presented by Wellington Mavhunduke during Week 10. Wellington currently works as a Fraud Analyst at Bankwest and previously worked as a SOC Analyst at Idoba. The lecture focused on how fraud and financial crime are detected, investigated and mitigated within the banking sector.The session explored different types of scams and emerging fraud trends currently affecting financial institutions and customers. Wellington discussed the tools and techniques used by fraud analysts to identify suspicious behaviour and monitor potential threats. One of the key topics covered was how artificial intelligence is increasingly being weaponised by attackers to create more convincing scams and fraudulent activities.

### How it Helped me

The lecture also provided insight into careers in fraud analysis and financial cybersecurity, including the skills and knowledge required to work in these areas. I found the session valuable because it connected cybersecurity concepts learned in class to real-world applications within the banking industry. It also helped me better understand how cybersecurity extends beyond technical systems into fraud prevention financial protection and customer security.

## 2) UCC Cybersecurity Tech Talk: Into The Cuckoo's Egg

I attended a cybersecurity tech talk hosted by the University Computer Club (UCC) titled Into The. " Cuckoo's Egg, presented by Mark Tearle. He works as a Senior Systems Administrator at QCIF. The talk discussed the famous cybersecurity incident involving a small accounting discrepancy at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory which eventually led to the discovery of a major cyber intrusion and espionage operation during the early days of the internet.

The presentation explored how the investigation was carried out, the methods used to track the attackers and the challenges faced during the incident response process. The talk highlighted the importance of persistence, system monitoring, logging and investigative thinking in cybersecurity. It also demonstrated how even minor anomalies within systems can sometimes indicate larger security threats.

### How it helped me

I found this talk particularly interesting because it showed how cybersecurity investigations were conducted before modern security tools existed and how many core principles of incident detection and analysis still remain relevant today. The session provided valuable insight into the history of cybersecurity and the evolution of cyber threat investigations.
