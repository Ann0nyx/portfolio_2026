# B6 - Attend 2 cyber ethics/law related talks/seminars

## 1) AI Security Guest Lecture(week 12)

I attended the Week 12 guest lecture on AI security presented by Larry Huynh during the unit lecture in the Arts block. The lecture focused on the role of artificial intelligence and machine learning in defensive cybersecurity, including topics such as anomaly-based intrusion detection systems (IDS), malware detection, large language models (LLMs) and AI-assisted vulnerability detection.

### What I learned

One of the key areas discussed was how machine learning models can be trained to detect malicious activity by analysing patterns in network traffic and malware samples. The lecture also explored ethical and security concerns surrounding AI systems, particularly how large language models can produce unsafe or harmful outputs if not properly trained. The speaker explained the importance of reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) in reducing harmful AI responses and improving safety mechanisms within modern AI systems.

Another important topic covered was the use of LLMs for code vulnerability detection. The lecture discussed both the advantages and limitations of relying on AI systems for cybersecurity tasks, emphasising that while AI can assist in identifying vulnerabilities, human oversight is still necessary because models may miss security flaws or produce incorrect results.I found this lecture valuable because it provided insight into the ethical and security implications of AI technologies and demonstrated how AI can both improve cybersecurity and introduce new risks if used irresponsibly.

Proof of Attendance was marked through the attendance sheet during lecture.

## 2) Blockchain and Law in Australia and Abroad Seminar

I attended a seminar hosted by Curtin University on Blockchain and Law in Australia and Abroad held on 28 April from 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM at 137 St Georges Terrace, Perth WA 6000. The seminar explored the legal, ethical, and regulatory challenges associated with blockchain technologies and cryptocurrencies in both Australia and international contexts.

### What I learned

The seminar discussed how blockchain systems operate as decentralised digital ledgers and examined the legal implications of emerging technologies such as cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, and digital assets. Topics included privacy concerns, regulation of blockchain-based systems, cybersecurity risks, and the challenges governments face when creating laws for rapidly evolving technologies.

The speakers also discussed how blockchain can impact industries such as finance, business, and digital identity systems in the future while highlighting ethical concerns surrounding misuse, fraud, and regulatory compliance. I found the seminar particularly interesting because it connected cybersecurity concepts with real-world legal and ethical issues, showing how technology, law, and security increasingly overlap in modern society.

The seminar improved my understanding of cyber law and ethics by demonstrating the importance of balancing innovation, privacy, security, and legal responsibility when developing and using emerging technologies.
